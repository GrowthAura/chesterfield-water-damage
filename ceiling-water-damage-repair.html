What causes a ceiling stain under an upstairs bathroom in a Chesterfield two-story?

Most often a leaking toilet seal, a failed shower pan or drain, cracked tub caulk, or a supply line. Water collects on top of the drywall and stains through once it is saturated.

Two-story homes here commonly place the primary bathroom over the kitchen or great room. A slow leak can run for weeks before the brown ring appears below.

Is it urgent?

Call right away if the ceiling is dripping, bulging or sagging, if water is near a light fixture or ceiling fan, or if the stain is growing by the hour. Turn off power to that fixture at the breaker and keep people out from under the bulge. A dry, old-looking stain that is not growing can be scheduled, but it still needs checking, because the leak that caused it may be ongoing.

Sources we trace

Bathrooms above

Toilet wax rings, shower pans, tub overflows, drain connections and supply valves.

Roof leaks

Hail and wind damage to shingles, failed flashing around chimneys and skylights, and nail pops.

Ice dams

In a hard freeze after snow, ice at the eaves forces meltwater back under shingles. Stains appear along exterior walls.

Attic HVAC

Many homes have a second furnace or air handler in the attic. A clogged condensate drain overflows the pan onto the ceiling below.

Pipes in the floor system

Frozen or failed supply lines between floors. See Burst Pipe Water Damage.

Why the cavity has to be dried

Patching and painting a stain without drying leaves wet insulation and framing sealed inside. That is how mold starts above a ceiling that looks fine. We open the wet section, remove soaked insulation, dry the joists and subfloor or roof deck, and only then close it.

Repair steps

Protect floors and furniture and set containment for dust.

Drain trapped water in a controlled way.

Remove sagging or saturated drywall and wet insulation.

Dry the cavity with air movers and dehumidifiers until readings are normal.

Hang new drywall, tape and finish.

Match the texture, whether smooth, knockdown or orange peel.

Prime with a stain-blocking primer and paint the full ceiling section so the repair does not show.

Vaulted and two-story ceilings

Great rooms with vaulted or two-story ceilings need scaffolding or lifts, and roof leaks there can travel down the slope before dripping. We track the wet area with thermal imaging before cutting so the opening is no larger than necessary.

Roof leak water damage

For roof leak water damage in Chesterfield, MO, the roof needs a roofer and the interior needs drying. We can tarp or advise on temporary protection, dry the attic and ceiling, and coordinate timing with your roofer so the interior repair is not done before the roof is sound.

Cost and insurance

A small section of ceiling with a simple source typically costs in the hundreds to low thousands of dollars to dry and repair. Large areas, high ceilings and texture matching add to it. Sudden leaks and storm damage are commonly covered by homeowners insurance. Long-term seepage usually is not.
