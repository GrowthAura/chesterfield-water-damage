Is Chesterfield Valley still at risk of flooding behind the Monarch-Chesterfield Levee?

The levee greatly reduces the risk of river flooding, but it does not remove it. Valley properties can also take on water from heavy rain, interior drainage backups and high groundwater when the Missouri River runs high.

A levee keeps the river out. It also keeps rainwater in until pumps and drainage channels move it. And when the river stays high for weeks, pressure pushes groundwater up under slabs on the protected side. So a Valley building can get wet without the river ever topping anything.

The three kinds of flooding we see

Flash flooding on hillside lots

In the subdivisions of 63017, intense rain runs downhill faster than yards and storm inlets can take it. Water pools against the back of the house and comes in at walkout doors, window wells and garage doors.

Creek flooding

Homes and common ground along Bonhomme Creek, Caulks Creek and Creve Coeur Creek can see water rise quickly after a few inches of rain.

Valley flooding and groundwater

In 63005, flat ground and a high water table mean parking lots, loading docks and slab floors take on water in prolonged wet periods.

Why flood cleanup is different

Water that has crossed the ground is Category 3. It carries soil, lawn chemicals, fuel residue and sewage. That means carpet, pad, soaked drywall and insulation are removed, not dried. Hard surfaces are washed and disinfected. Framing is cleaned and dried before anything is closed up.

Our flood restoration process

Pump out standing water and extract what remains.

Shovel and wash out mud and silt.

Remove flooring, drywall and insulation that floodwater touched, cutting walls above the water line.

Pressure-wash and disinfect slabs, framing and remaining surfaces.

Dry the structure with dehumidifiers and air movers, tracked with daily readings.

Rebuild walls, floors and finishes.

Safety after a flood

Do not enter a flooded building until power is confirmed off. Assume floodwater is contaminated and wear boots and gloves. Do not use gas appliances or the furnace until they are inspected. Throw out food that touched floodwater. Watch for displaced wildlife in debris. Photograph everything before you move it.

Storm damage beyond rising water

Severe storms also send water in from above: wind-driven rain through damaged roofing, broken windows and failed flashing. Storm damage restoration in Chesterfield, MO often involves tarping, ceiling removal and attic drying along with ground-level cleanup. See Ceiling Water Damage Repair.

Flood insurance versus homeowners insurance

Homeowners policies exclude rising water from outside. That is covered by a separate flood policy, through the National Flood Insurance Program or a private insurer. Wind-driven rain that enters through storm-damaged roofing is typically a homeowners claim. Sewer backup during a storm falls under a backup endorsement. One storm can involve all three, and the cause of each area of damage matters. We document each one separately. See Water Damage Insurance Claim.

How long it takes and what it costs

Mud-out and removal take one to several days. Drying takes about a week because framing has been fully saturated. Rebuilding follows. Costs vary widely with the depth of water and how much of the building was finished. A garage or unfinished area is at the low end, in the low thousands. A finished floor of a home or a commercial suite is considerably more. A written scope is provided before work proceeds.
