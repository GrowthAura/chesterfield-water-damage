Why do finished basements in Chesterfield flood even on high ground above the valley?

Because the soil is heavy clay. It holds rainwater against foundation walls, pressure builds, and water enters through floor cracks, wall joints, window wells and walkout door thresholds when drainage cannot keep up.

Being far from the Missouri River does not protect a house on a hillside lot. Runoff from the yard above flows toward the back of the house, which is exactly where the walkout door and lower-level windows are.

The five ways water gets into a Chesterfield basement

Sump pump failure

The most common cause. A power outage, a stuck float or a worn-out pump lets the pit overflow. See Sump Pump Failure Cleanup.

Hydrostatic pressure

Saturated clay pushes water through the joint where the floor meets the wall and up through hairline slab cracks. It often shows as a wet line around the room perimeter.

Window wells and walkout doors

A clogged window well drain fills like a fish tank until the window leaks or breaks. Walkout doors take water when the patio drain is blocked with leaves.

Sewer backup through the floor drain

Water that comes up from a drain is contaminated and is handled differently. See Sewage Backup Cleanup.

Plumbing above

A failed water heater, a burst pipe or an overflowing fixture upstairs ends up in the basement because water runs downhill.

Our basement cleanup process

Pump-out and extraction

Deep water is pumped. Carpet, pad and hard floors are extracted. Details are on our Emergency Water Extraction page.

Deciding what stays and what goes

In a finished basement the question is always the same: what can be dried in place? Carpet can often be saved after clean water. Pad is usually replaced. Drywall that wicked water is cut out a foot or two above the water line, a method called a flood cut, so the wall cavity can dry. Wet fiberglass insulation is removed because it does not dry in a closed wall.

Cleaning

Floors, wall plates and exposed framing are cleaned and treated with an antimicrobial product to discourage mold growth.

Drying

Air movers push dry air across wet surfaces and dehumidifiers pull the moisture out of the air. Readings are taken daily at the same points until framing and concrete are back to a normal range.

Rebuild

Insulation, drywall, trim, paint and flooring are replaced so the room looks the way it did.

Finished basement features that need extra care

Home theaters: acoustic panels and carpeted risers trap water and are checked individually.

Wet bars and kitchenettes: cabinet toe-kicks hide water. We drill small holes behind the kick plate to dry the cavity or remove the base if it is swollen.

Basement bedrooms and offices: closets and built-ins block airflow and are opened up.

Bathrooms: water travels under tile and tubs, so adjoining walls are metered.

Mechanical rooms: furnaces and water heaters that sat in water should be checked by an HVAC contractor before use.

How long does basement water removal take?

Pumping and extraction are usually finished on the first visit. Drying takes three to five days in most homes, longer if water sat for more than a day or reached framing behind finished walls. Repairs follow once the readings are dry. Basement water removal in Chesterfield, MO done in that order avoids sealing moisture into new drywall.

What makes one basement job cost more than another

Square footage, depth of water, whether the space is finished, whether the water is clean or contaminated, and how long it sat. An unfinished basement with clean water is at the low end, often in the hundreds to low thousands. A fully finished lower level that needs flood cuts, new pad and repairs runs from several thousand dollars upward. The written scope breaks this down room by room.

Keeping it from happening again

We are not a waterproofing company, but we see the same causes repeatedly and will point them out: downspouts that discharge next to the foundation, window wells without covers, a sump pump with no battery backup, grading that slopes toward the house. Fixing those is usually cheaper than a second basement flood repair in Chesterfield, MO.
