Why water spreads so quickly in West County homes

Builders in this part of St. Louis County finished basements with carpet over pad, drywall down to the slab and wood trim. All three act like a wick. An inch of water on the floor can climb a foot or more up the wall within a day. Clay soil outside keeps the foundation damp long after the rain stops, so a basement that looks dry on the surface can still read wet inside the wall cavity.

This is why water damage repair in Chesterfield, MO usually involves more than a shop vacuum and a fan. Proper drying means measuring moisture in the materials, removing baseboards so air can reach the wall base, and running dehumidifiers sized for the room until the readings come down.

What to do before the crew arrives

  • Shut off the water at the main valve if a pipe or fixture is the source.
  • Turn off power to the wet area at the breaker if you can reach the panel without standing in water.
  • Stay out of water that came from a floor drain, toilet or outside flooding. It is contaminated.
  • Move electronics, rugs, documents and small furniture to a dry room.
  • Put foil or plastic under furniture legs that sit on wet carpet.
  • Take photos and short videos of every affected room before anything is moved. Your adjuster will ask for them.
  • Do not run the furnace fan or central air through wet rooms if you suspect mold or sewage.

Working with your insurance company

Most policies cover sudden and accidental water, such as a burst pipe or a failed water heater.
