How long does it take to dry out walls in a Chesterfield home after a leak?

Usually three to five days with professional equipment. Hardwood, plaster, concrete and framing soaked for more than a day can take a week or longer. Moisture readings decide the end point.

Without equipment, walls here can stay wet for weeks, particularly in summer when outdoor air is as humid as the room you are trying to dry.

What structural drying means

Building materials absorb water like a sponge. Drywall, wood studs, subfloor, concrete and insulation hold it long after the carpet feels dry. Structural drying removes that bound water by controlling three things: airflow across wet surfaces, low humidity in the room, and temperature. Get those right and water moves out of the materials and into the air, where the dehumidifier collects it.

The equipment

Air movers

Low, high-speed fans that push air along walls and floors to lift moisture off surfaces.

LGR dehumidifiers

Low-grain refrigerant units that keep pulling water from the air even when humidity is already low, which household dehumidifiers cannot do.

Wall and cavity drying systems

Small hoses feed dry air through holes behind the baseboard into wall cavities, under cabinets and into ceilings.

Floor mats

Sealed panels that draw moisture up through hardwood. See Hardwood Floor Water Damage.

Moisture meters and thermal cameras

Pin and pinless meters measure moisture in materials. Thermal imaging shows wet areas as cooler patches.

Moisture mapping

On day one we mark every wet area on a sketch of the affected rooms and record a reading at each point. We also take a reading from a dry part of the house as the target, called the dry standard. Each day the same points are re-measured. You can see the numbers drop, and so can your adjuster.

Why daily checks matter

Equipment is adjusted as the job progresses. Air movers are repositioned, a dehumidifier is added or removed, and a wall that is not responding is opened. Leaving machines untouched for a week wastes time and electricity, and can leave a wet pocket behind.

What we open and what we leave

Baseboards usually come off. Wet pad comes out. Drywall stays if it is drying and sound. Insulation that is soaked is removed. The aim is to save as much as can be dried properly, because every material saved is one that does not have to be rebuilt.

Living with the equipment

It is loud, about like a row of box fans on high, and it warms the room. Keep doors and windows in the drying area closed so the dehumidifiers are not trying to dry the outdoors. Leave the machines running around the clock. The electricity used over a typical job is modest compared with the cost of the repairs it prevents.

When drying is not enough

Materials contaminated by sewage or floodwater are removed, not dried. Mold that has already formed needs remediation. See Mold Remediation. Standing water has to be extracted first. See Emergency Water Extraction.

Cost and insurance

Drying is priced by the equipment needed and the number of days. A single room is typically in the hundreds to low thousands of dollars. Several rooms or a full lower level cost more. When the water loss is covered, drying is part of the claim, and the daily log supports it. Once dry, repairs are covered on Water Damaged Drywall Repair.
