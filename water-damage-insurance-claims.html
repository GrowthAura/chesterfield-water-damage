Does homeowners insurance in Missouri cover a flooded basement in Chesterfield?

It depends on the cause. Sudden pipe or appliance failures are usually covered. Sump overflow and sewer backup need a separate endorsement. Rising water from outside requires flood insurance. Slow seepage is generally excluded.

We are a restoration crew, not insurance agents or adjusters. What follows is general information. Your policy and your insurer decide coverage.

Cause decides coverage

Usually covered

A burst pipe. A water heater that fails. A washing machine hose that splits. A sudden toilet or tub overflow. Rain entering through storm damage to the roof.

Covered only with an added endorsement

Water that backs up through a sewer or drain. Water that overflows from a sump pit. This endorsement is inexpensive and worth having in any home with a finished basement and a sump pump.

Covered by a flood policy

Surface water, creek overflow and river flooding entering from outside.

Usually not covered

Groundwater seeping through foundation walls. Leaks that went on for weeks or months. Damage from lack of maintenance. Mold not connected to a covered loss.

What to do in the first day

Stop the water and prevent further damage. Policies require you to take reasonable steps, and insurers pay for them.

Photograph and video everything before it is moved.

Call your insurer or agent and get a claim number.

Keep damaged items until the adjuster has seen them or approved disposal. Photos are enough for contaminated materials that must go.

Keep receipts for anything you spend.

Do not wait for the adjuster before starting extraction and drying. Delay makes damage worse and can itself become a coverage problem.

What we document

The source and cause, with photos of the failed part where possible.

The category of water.

Every affected room and material, on a moisture map.

Daily moisture readings showing drying progress.

Equipment used and for how long.

Materials removed and why.

An itemized scope for repairs.

This is the water damage claim documentation an adjuster needs to approve the work without repeated visits.

Working with the adjuster

We can meet the adjuster on site and walk through the damage, or send the file electronically. If the adjuster's scope differs from ours, we explain the readings behind our recommendations. You stay in control of the claim. You choose who does the work, and you approve the scope.

Deductibles and limits

Your deductible applies to each claim. Backup endorsements and mold coverage often have their own lower limits, commonly a fixed amount chosen when the policy was written. Check your declarations page so there are no surprises.
