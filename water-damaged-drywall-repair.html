Does wet drywall in a Chesterfield basement always have to be cut out?

No. Drywall wetted briefly by clean water can often be dried in place. It is cut out when it has swollen or softened, when insulation behind it is soaked, or when the water was contaminated.

In finished basements here, exterior walls are usually insulated, and wet insulation is the deciding factor. It will not dry inside a closed wall, so the lower section comes out.

What a flood cut is

A flood cut is a straight, level cut through the drywall above the wet line, usually 12 to 24 inches above the highest wet reading, sometimes at 4 feet to match the sheet seam. The lower section is removed. This opens the cavity for drying and leaves a clean line for the new drywall to meet.

Behind the wall

Insulation

Wet fiberglass batts are removed and replaced. Wet insulation loses its value and holds moisture against the studs.

Vapor barrier

Some basement walls have plastic sheeting behind the drywall. It traps water and is cut back so the wall can dry.

Framing

Studs and bottom plates are cleaned, treated and dried until they read normal. Building over wet framing causes mold.

Wiring and outlets

Outlets that were submerged should be checked or replaced by an electrician before the wall is closed.

The rebuild

New insulation is fitted.

Drywall is hung, with moisture-resistant board used in bathrooms and other damp locations.

Seams are taped and finished in three coats.

Texture is matched to the existing wall.

The wall is primed and painted corner to corner so the color is uniform.

Baseboards, casing and doors are reinstalled or replaced.

Trim and baseboards

Most homes here have MDF or finger-jointed pine baseboards. MDF swells when wet and is replaced. Solid wood trim can often be dried and reused. We match the profile as closely as available stock allows.

Drying comes first

Water damage reconstruction in Chesterfield, MO should not start until moisture readings confirm the framing is dry. Closing a wall early is the most common cause of mold after a repair. The drying phase is described on our Structural Drying page. If mold is found when the wall is opened, see Mold Remediation.

Ceilings and floors

Ceiling repairs are covered on Ceiling Water Damage Repair. Flooring follows the walls so new floors are not damaged during finishing.

Matching the existing finish

New paint rarely matches old paint exactly, even from the same can, because walls fade. That is why we paint whole walls. For textured walls and ceilings, a test patch is done first.

Cost and insurance

Repairing a small area of drywall typically costs from a few hundred dollars to around two thousand. A full basement perimeter with insulation, trim and paint is several thousand. When the water loss is covered, wall repair is normally included in the claim.
